The Meaning of Time

Beyond the clocks. Beyond the measurements. Beyond the moment.

What is time?

We have spent centuries trying to understand it. Scientists have measured it, philosophers have questioned it and astronomers have traced it through the movement of the heavens.

Each perspective brings us closer to understanding something that remains profoundly mysterious.

A universe in motion

Time existed long before humanity. It is written into the movement of galaxies, the lives of stars, the turning of our planet and the changing of the seasons.

We have built extraordinary instruments to measure its passage, yet the universe operates on scales almost beyond imagination.

The universe itself has a history. But whether time had a beginning, and what that might mean, remains one of the deepest questions in cosmology.

Our lives occupy just a fleeting moment in this vast unfolding of events.

The human experience

But time is more than something measured by clocks or described by physics.

It is memory, anticipation and awareness. It is the feeling of an afternoon disappearing, a childhood remembered or a moment we wish could last forever.

Time seems to have a direction. We remember the past, anticipate the future and watch the world change irreversibly around us. Why does time appear to move forward? Even physics has profound questions to answer.

We experience time differently from how we measure it. And perhaps that difference is where its greatest mystery lies.

The present moment

We cannot return to the past or step into the future. We experience life here, in the continuous unfolding of the present.

A moment of your life.

Already gone. Never to return. And yet, here you are.

We measure time. We study it. We try to understand it.

But perhaps the most interesting question isn’t what time is.

It is what it means to be alive within it.

And, ultimately, what we choose to do with the time we have.

The universe has its own time. We have ours. And somewhere between the two, we find ourselves.

A note on this page

Newton set out his idea of absolute time in the Principia of 1687. Einstein’s special and general theories of relativity date from 1905 and 1915. Bergson wrote about lived time, which he called duration, in Time and Free Will in 1889. These are the commonly given accounts and were not individually checked for this page. Whether time had a beginning, and why it seems to run in one direction, are open questions, and the page offers them as questions. The counter measures only how long this page has been open in front of you. The small drawings beside each name are illustrations of the idea, not diagrams of the physics, and the stars are decoration, not a map of the sky.
